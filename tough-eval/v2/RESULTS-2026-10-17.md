# GinBench: results, 17 October 2026

GinBench measures how well a model applies a legal team's written playbook to contracts: whether it finds the departures, raises false alarms, or invents anything. This is the summary of the first frozen run, plus the preliminary layers around it. Its code is in `outputs/tough-eval/`, which keeps that name until the next benchmark version because the frozen benchmark's file hashes are keyed on its paths.

## What was tested

| Layer | Question | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Planted deviations** | Does the model apply our playbook to our kind of paper? Common Paper CSA and NDA, four sides. The deviations were written by three model families (Opus, GLM, DeepSeek), and each one was checked by the other two. | **Frozen** (tag `bench-v2.0-a`), run once per setup |
| H. History | Does the agent use past deals from the document store correctly, and resist being misled? | Preliminary |
| B. Real NDAs | Does it hold up on unfamiliar third-party paper? (ContractNLI test split, outside labels.) | Preliminary; labels disputed |
| C. Attorney fixes | Do its proposed changes match an attorney's? (Adapted from RedlineBench.) | Not used for comparison |

## Headline: layer A, frozen

28 planted deviations and 47 harmless edits across 75 single items, plus 3 multi-change markups.

| Setup | Deviations missed | Upper bound (95%) | Reviews with an invented quote | False alarms per agreement | Fixed (of caught) | All-pass, single items | Failed reviews |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Opus 5.5**, product prompt | **0 of 28** | 10.2% | **0 of 78** | **0.08** | 27 of 28 | **66 of 75** | 0 |
| **DeepSeek V4 Flash**, product prompt | **0 of 28** | 10.2% | 1 of 75 | 0.11 | 27 of 28 | 65 of 75 | 3 of 78 |
| Opus 5.5, plain prompt | 1 of 28 | 15.8% | 0 of 78 | 0 | 24 of 27 | 64 of 75 | 0 |
| GLM-5.3 Flash, product prompt | 4 of 28 | 29.8% | 0 of 75 | 0.04 | 21 of 24 | 61 of 75 | 3 of 78 |
| Mistral Small 4, product prompt | 20 of 28 | | 13 of 78 | 2.96 | 4 of 8 | 17 of 75 | 0 |
| *GLM-5.3 Flash, larger output budget (declared variant, not frozen)* | 2 of 28 | 20.8% | 1 of 78 | 0.12 | 23 of 26 | 57 of 75 | 0 |

**What it says:**
- **Both Opus and DeepSeek apply the playbook almost perfectly.** Neither missed a planted deviation, both fixed 27 of 28, and both got about 9 in 10 agreements fully right.
- **Opus's remaining edge is reliability,** not accuracy: it invented no quotes and no review failed, against one invented quote and three failed reviews for DeepSeek.
- **DeepSeek ran through an EU-hosted endpoint,** so it's a credible option for a team that can't send contracts to a US provider.
- **GLM-5.3 Flash is precise but misses more:** 4 of 28 missed, all vendor-side, with the fewest false alarms of any model (0.04). Three reviews failed because it ran out of output room on the long contracts. Given twice the output budget (a variant declared before its results), it missed 2, none failed, but it raised more false alarms.
- **Mistral Small 4 is not usable:** it missed 20 of 28 and invented quotes in 13 of 78 reviews.
- **Our product prompt beats a plain prompt:** the plain prompt missed one deviation and fixed fewer.

**Why these numbers are higher than the preliminary run:** in the preliminary run, Opus raised about one false alarm per agreement and got 33 of 75 fully right. Reading every false alarm showed that almost all of them came from our own test materials, not the model:
- five clauses of the "clean" contract actually tripped a playbook rule;
- a Cover Page line became untrue after a planted change.

We calibrated the clean contracts with three model families, fixed those materials and three scorer bugs, then froze and re-ran. Opus didn't change; the test did. The changes are recorded in `LAYER-A-FIXES.md`.

## History: layer H (preliminary, Opus only)

25 scenarios. The agent reads a document store holding earlier deals, emails and lawyers' notes.

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Deviations missed | 0 of 25 |
| Precedent cited where needed | 8 of 8 |
| Bait concessions followed | 0 of 9 |
| Citations attributed to the wrong counterparty | 0 of 25 |
| Planted instructions followed | 0 of 4 |
| Citations that could not be verified | 0 |

These runs predate the layer A fixes, so their false alarms (and a low all-pass of 6 of 25) come from the same test-material problems.

## Robustness: layer B (preliminary, labels disputed)

On 123 real NDAs, Opus with the product prompt and Qwen 3.8 27B missed the fewest labelled departures (23 of 154 each), and Opus invented nothing. But on reading, about a quarter of the labels don't fit our playbook, for every model. With the disputed labels set aside, the top setups all reach zero or near-zero misses, so this layer can't separate them. It also spent its single look at the test split; the frozen version will use a fresh sample. Details: `analysis/audit-B-C-2026-10-17.md`.

## Not used: layer C

An empty answer scores 10 of 22, only two criteria separate the setups, and the judges are drawn from the contestants. It's reported but not used to rank anything.

## Caveats

- **One frozen run per setup.** Run-to-run variation isn't measured yet.
- **The deviations are written by models,** not by opposing lawyers. They're checked to be real playbook breaches, but may be cleaner than a real counterparty's drafting.
- **Same-family readings.** The error analyses and audits were done with Claude models. Their conclusions about disputed labels in particular need an outside check.
- **No lawyer has validated** the playbooks, the labels or the readings.
- **The product has moved on since the freeze.** The app on `main` has changed since `bench-v2.0-a`. These results describe the tagged version.
