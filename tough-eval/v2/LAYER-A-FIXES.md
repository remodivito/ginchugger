# Layer A fixes before the frozen run (17 October 2026)

This file records each fix, the clean-copy calibration (P8) that was skipped in the preliminary run, the re-application of the items, and the layer A freeze (tag `bench-v2.0-a`). No lawyer has checked any of it; every reading below is ours, made against the playbook text, and the playbooks and these fixes were written with Claude, the family of the reference setup.

## Fix 2: the Cover Page consequence of NDA plants on the Standard Terms

The Common Paper Mutual NDA's Cover Page says "Changes to Standard Terms: None." (p10) and "Neither party has changed the Standard Terms…" (p12). An item that edits or inserts into the attached Standard Terms (any paragraph after the "Standard Terms" heading, p13) makes both lines untrue, and a reviewer who says so is right (19 of Opus's 81 preliminary product-prompt false alarms). Such plants now carry `secondaryTargets: ["p10", "p12"]` (`lib/items.mjs` `coverPageSecondary`, set by `lib/store.mjs` `plantOf`). In `score.mjs` a flag sitting on a secondary target is not a false alarm, does not count as a flag on a benign edit (even when it quotes the benign paragraph), and does not locate or catch anything; each agreement reports these flags as `secondaryFlags`. It applies to deviation and benign plants alike, since both change the Standard Terms. Plants on the Cover Page itself (an insertion after p10) and CSA plants get none.

## Fix 3: the plain prompt names the side

`arms/plain.md` said "for the Customer" on every side, and Opus declined a vendor-side review because of it (the plain prompt's only preliminary layer A miss). It now reads "for the {side} against the {side}'s playbook" and "the {side}'s positions". `review-one.mjs` already filled `{side}` from the playbook's `party`; the filling moved to `arms/render-prompt.mjs` (testable without running a review), whose fallback for a playbook naming no side (the balanced ContractNLI NDA playbook) is now "client", the product prompt's own word, where it was the ungrammatical "the party whose playbook this is". The four sides render as Customer, Provider, Disclosing Party and Receiving Party. The product prompt (`contract-review/prompts/review.md`) is unchanged: SHA-256 `30fe874f…`, as in every preliminary run.

## Fix 4: inserted clauses fixed by acceptable wording

An inserted clause was fixed only by deleting it. Now it is also fixed by a proposal for the inserted paragraph that passes its `acceptCheck` in `inserted-checks.json`: a `complies` check (require, forbid, num with min and max, as every other fix check) written from the rule's preferred or acceptable position on what the clause inserts, so wording at that position is a fix and wording still at the escalation tier or hard line is not. Every acceptCheck must fail on the planted clause and pass on its own `acceptReference` (`selftest.mjs --suite v2a`, `tests/layer-a-fixes.test.mjs`). An inserted clause with no entry is fixed only by deletion. There are seven entries, one for each inserted-clause item (all verified): three C-19 audit clauses (acceptable: usage audits on 30 days' notice, once a year, at the Provider's cost; no system or on-site access), one V-03 uncapped confidentiality clause (preferred: confidentiality inside the caps; acceptable: unlimited claims extended mutually to gross negligence, wilful misconduct and fraud), two NDA-18 non-solicitation clauses (mutual, employees met through the discussions, at most 12 months, general advertising excluded, no no-hire) and one NDA-11 reverse-engineering clause (limited to samples, prototypes or software provided under the MNDA). Forbid patterns are negation-aware, because a carve-out ("without access to Customer's systems"; "Nothing in this MNDA restricts … commercially available products") is the compliant wording. Disclosure: the checks were written by Claude Opus 5.5. After the first draft they were run against the preliminary runs' proposals on these items, and two forbid patterns were made negation-aware because they rejected compliant carve-outs. Against the preliminary proposals, every non-deletion proposal now passes, including those from Opus product, Opus plain, GLM and DeepSeek. Each one reads as acceptable-tier wording.

## Fix 5: typographic normalisation in the quote check

`review.js` (the product) verifies a quote as an exact substring, so a straight apostrophe against the document's curly one came back `verified: false` and counted as invented (2 in the Opus empty-record run, 2 GLM layer A). On v2 suites other than the preliminary snapshot, `score.mjs` now re-checks each unverified quote against the cited paragraph of the variant (any paragraph when none is cited) after normalising curly and straight quotes and apostrophes, the dash characters (hyphen, en and em dash, minus) and runs of whitespace including non-breaking spaces (`typo`, `reverifyQuotes`). A match counts as verified, and every agreement reports the number as `quotesNormalised`. Case, words and ellipses are not normalised. The product is unchanged. Layer B (`contractnli/score.mjs`) has its own gate and is not changed here.

## Re-application of the items

`apply.mjs` was re-run on all twelve author batches (no model call: the stored author answers and their stored resubmissions, `noCall`), then `markups.mjs`, so `items.json`, `markups.json`, `variants/` and `manifest.json` are rebuilt on the amended base. Every item still applies. The item set is identical in content to the preliminary one: 144 items (96 deviations, 48 benign), statuses unchanged (verified: 28 deviations and 47 benign; contested: 68 deviations and 1 benign), the same edits, fixes and verification records, and the same 3 markups. No item's planted paragraph changed (no item edits CSA p19), so no item changed meaning and none needed re-verification. Variant bytes: the 74 CSA variants (singles and the two CSA markups) differ only in p19's new text, and the 73 NDA variants are byte-identical. The manifest is item set version `v2a-1`. It carries `secondaryTargets` on 55 variants (NDA plants on the Standard Terms) and `acceptCheck` on 8 (the seven inserted-clause items and the markup M-opus-csa-vendor, which contains one of them).

As a diagnostic only (not a result), the preliminary runs' outputs on the 40 byte-identical NDA variants were rescored with fixes 2, 4 and 5. False alarms fall from 19 to 1 (Opus product), 8 to 1 (DeepSeek) and 12 to 5 (GLM). All-pass rises from 18 to 34 of 40 (Opus product), 35 to 38 (Opus plain), 29 to 36 (DeepSeek) and 25 to 32 (GLM). Fixed deviations rise by 1 to 3 per setup.
