### B2. Scoring mechanics

- **Wrong-rule citations count as misses.** Misses where a departs flag sits on a gold evidence paragraph but cites another rule: Opus product 3 (N005→NDA-05/07, N354→NDA-04/02, N452→NDA-20), plain 2, Qwen 4, DeepSeek 6, GLM 9, Mistral 26. For GLM and Mistral many of these flags carry **no rule id at all**: GLM has 218 untagged flags (23% of its flags), Mistral 149. So part of their miss rate is a tagging and format failure, not a reading failure. That should be reported apart.
- **Over-tagging inflates recall.** A finding counts as a catch for every rule it lists (`contractnli/score.mjs` line 57: `on = flags.filter(f => (f.ruleIds||[]).includes(rule))`). Catches where every supporting flag has 2 or more tags: Opus 35 of 131, Qwen 55, DeepSeek 32, GLM 39, plain 32. With 3 or more tags: Opus 2, Qwen 11, DeepSeek 5, GLM 9, plain 4, Mistral 3. Catches where the rule is never a supporting flag's *first* tag: Opus 6, plain 9, GLM 20, DeepSeek 23, Qwen 36.

  | Setup | Catches counting only first-listed tags | Misses |
  |---|---|---|
  | Opus product | 125/154 | 29 |
  | Opus plain | 109/154 | 45 |
  | GLM | 103/154 | 51 |
  | DeepSeek | 95/154 | 59 |
  | Qwen | 95/154 | 59 |

  First-tag order is not a defined field, so this is only a sensitivity check. It still shows that the Opus–Qwen tie depends on counting secondary tags.
- **Coincidental catches.** Three of my ten sampled Opus catches (B3) were credit for a flag about something else that happened to carry the labelled rule's id. Examples: an NDA-03 "visual disclosures" counter tagged NDA-01, and a punctuation fix on an acceptable non-solicit.
- **"Departs" includes `accept-with-modification` and `decision: "none"`.** Under `review.md` line 11, accept-with-modification means "the outcome is established but different wording is needed", which is not a departure. Catches resting only on accept-with-modification flags: DeepSeek 31, Qwen 15, GLM 13, Mistral 4, Opus 2. GLM has 204 attention/none flags, which feed its flag counts.
- **"Located"** (`touches`, `contractnli/score.mjs` line 34) is satisfied by the finding's own paragraph or any verified evidence paragraph. It sits at 90–99% for every setup, so it carries almost no signal. As the README says, it measures agreement with the annotator's span choice.
- **Silence and silentOk.** The six silentOk rules have 386 silent pairs and 1 departure between them. False alarms are therefore measured only where recall cannot be, and recall only where false alarms are not scored. Stricter-than-label and omission flags are unscored. For Opus product they come to 2.98 and 1.92 per NDA, against 0.46 scored false alarms.
- **Double counting.** One flag can be a catch on one rule and a false alarm on another at the same time (the NDA-20 plus NDA-16 tag). Nothing is double counted inside a single rule.
- **Failures.** Failed reviews count as "nothing found" (`contractnli/score.mjs` line 46). GLM's 2 failures (N521: length; N537: broken JSON) supply 3 of its 31 misses. That is consistent with the spec, but the write-up's B table has no failures column.
- **Explicit "ok" exists only in the product prompt.** All 23 Opus-product misses carry an explicit within-guidance finding on the rule (Qwen 22, DeepSeek 25, GLM 24). The plain prompt is told "Do not comment on provisions that comply" (`arms/plain.md`), so it cannot do this. The prompts differ in what a reviewer can say, not only in style.

### B4. Representativeness and leakage

- **Memorisation.** ContractNLI (2021) is public on GitHub and Hugging Face, and its documents are EDGAR exhibits and web PDFs. Every model has plausibly seen texts and labels. The P11 probe was not run. Label memorisation would help a model match the label, including the wrong labels. Nothing measures this.
- **Fit to an in-house team.** 47 of 123 test NDAs are SEC filings (`sec-text` 24, `sec-html` 23), mostly 1996–2019 M&A confidentiality letters with standstills and non-solicits. 76 are web PDFs: templates, government, university, Indian and South African forms. Roughly 23 of the 28 NDA-18 departures come from SEC M&A letters. 13 departure pairs come from documents that are not NDAs: N395 and N444 (asset-purchase non-compete, the same contract twice), N408, N412, N432, N596, N549 (an employee covenant) and N452 (an independent-contractor agreement). This is not the mix a UK software vendor's legal team signs, and the write-up should say so.
- **Test-split contamination during development.** Before 16 October no run touched the test split: every earlier `cnli-*` run is dev-only (61), and the pool has never been run. The NDA-11 playbook change was made on dev. **Since the preliminary runs, however, the test split has been read item by item (both error analyses and this audit), and fixes are planned from it.** See executive risk 2.
- **Duplicates.** A minhash plus exact 6-gram Jaccard over all 607 documents found these near-duplicates inside the test split: N052/N097 (1.00), N537/N539 (1.00), N395/N444 (0.99) and N049/N053 (0.91). Each pair counts as two clusters. Across splits there are 15 pairs, 14 of them involving test. One is test–dev (N565 test / N586 dev, 0.70). The rest are test–pool: N395/N391, N537/N538, N539/N538, N400/N399, N549/N515 and N549/N548 (≥0.96), N496/N502, N471/N529, N496/N287, N606/N518, N042/N560 and N004/N104. **Any fresh sample from the pool must drop these.** Label consistency on the near-identical pairs: N537/N539 disagree on NDA-01 (C/N) and N049/N053 on NDA-19 (N/E). That is a direct measure of annotation noise: 2 disagreements in 51 rule pairs on near-identical text.

### B5. Statistics and sensitivity

- **Clustering.** `stats.mjs` `clusteredRate` bootstraps over NDAs that have at least one departure: 86 clusters, not 123 (37 test NDAs carry none). It falls back to Clopper–Pearson when misses are zero. That is correct as far as it goes. The near-duplicate pairs should be single clusters (small effect).
- **What the bound ignores.** It treats the labels as truth and one run as the setup's behaviour. Label noise (22–31% of the denominator) and run noise (6 of 51 self-disagreements for Opus on identical text) both exceed the sampling error the bootstrap reports. "Misses fewer than 1 in 5 with 95% confidence" holds only conditional on the published labels and that one run.
- **Opus versus Qwen.** On raw labels they are discordant on 20 departures, 10 each way (exact McNemar p = 1.0): no evidence of a difference. The tie is also not evidence of equivalence: the difference's interval is roughly ±6 points.

## Layer C

### C7. Our method against RedlineBench's, adaptation by adaptation

RedlineBench (`eval-feasibility/redline-bench/README.md`, Metrics): the agent produces a `.docx` with native tracked changes and comments. There is a validity gate: at least one tracked change or comment, otherwise reward 0. A three-judge panel from families outside every benchmarked model (gpt-5.4-mini, claude-haiku-4-5, gemini-3.1-flash-lite) votes by strict majority on each rubric. Rubrics are weighted 1–10, with negative-weight penalties in some tasks. reward = clamp(Σearned − Σpenalties) / Σpositive, averaged within each input group across the attorney variants, then over groups.

| # | Our adaptation | Likely direction of bias |
|---|---|---|

### C8. Why the empty output scores 10/22, and what range is informative

The empty output passes exactly the 10 keep criteria (s1: 01, 04, 07, 09, 13; s2: 01, 02; s3: 02, 03, 07) and fails all 12 change criteria. All four control judges are unanimous (`results/v2/C/control/empty`). Decomposing the setups:

| Setup | Keep (of 10) | Change (of 12) | Change criteria passed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Empty | 10 | 0 | none |
| Opus product | 8 | 5 (6 with the hidden fix) | s1-05, s1-10, s3-01, s3-04, s3-06 (+ s1-06) |
| Opus plain | 8 | 5 | s1-10, s3-01, s3-04, s3-05, s3-06 |
| DeepSeek | 8 | 4 | s1-10, s3-01, s3-04, s3-06 |
| GLM (s3 only) | 2 | 3 | s3-01, s3-04, s3-06 |

- **Every** setup loses 2 keep criteria to the empty output: s3-03, and s3-07 by restoring "everywhere". So the total "13 vs 10" is really "+5 change, −2 keep".
- Three of the four s3 change criteria are passed by everyone. Of s1's 8 change criteria, everyone passes s1-10 and nobody except Opus product passes anything else.
- **The informative range is the 12 change criteria, and in practice 2 of them** (s1-05 and s3-05). Everything else is passed by all setups or failed by all. s2 is non-informative. The keep criteria work only as penalties and should be shown as "harmful edits: 2 of 10".

### C10. Keep, fix or drop?

**In its current form, drop C from any comparative claim.** It has three documents. One (s2) cannot discriminate. One (s3) is mostly passed by everyone. Between setups the differences are one criterion, decided by judge choice or a renderer bug. The write-up's "indicative only" framing is right, but the single-number table invites a ranking.

Minimum changes to make it informative:
1. Report change criteria passed and keep criteria broken separately (spec section 7 already requires this). Remove the single total.
2. One fixed panel for every contestant, from families not under test, or all judges on all outputs with own-family votes excluded and per-judge results published. Validity-control every voting judge, the tiebreaker included.
3. Extend the control with partial and alternative-wording outputs, for example the golden with one change removed, or a paraphrased fix, so the control tests discrimination rather than "nothing versus everything".
4. Fix `render.mjs` to show every finding that carries wording, or document the product behaviour and apply it to both prompts.
5. Replace s2 (acceptance-only) with a task that has change criteria. Add tasks: at least 8–10 input groups, so that differences of 3 or more criteria could mean something.
6. Re-word C-s3-05 to follow the attorney's justification ("worldwide", not "everywhere"), or drop it as coupled to C-s3-07.
7. Apply the attorney weights, at least as a second line.

If those cannot be done before the freeze, keep C only as a qualitative appendix ("what the fixes look like"), with no table.

## General (question 11)

- **Prompt asymmetry, product versus plain.** Plain has no tier vocabulary, may not mark compliance, and is hard-coded "for the Customer" (`arms/plain.md` line 1). On B that wording is harmless because the playbook is mutual. On A it caused a miss. The product prompt in third-party mode says to "treat every deviation from the client's positions as a change to propose" (`review.md` Method). That drives preferred-position flags, which B's recall rewards and B's false-alarm metric mostly ignores. Product versus plain on B is therefore partly a measure of flagging appetite.
- **Output caps and failure classes.** Open models run with `max_completion_tokens` 64,000 including reasoning. The Claude CLI runs with no explicit cap. `retry-policy.mjs` classes "Claude CLI failed" as transport (retried indefinitely), while open-model truncation is malformed and becomes a scored failure after three attempts. Matched caps and a common classification are needed before any failure-rate comparison.
- **Effort.** Claude runs at `medium`. Munito models run at provider default (`effortApplies: false`). Gemini runs at `high` baked into the model id. These are not matched and not described as matched.
- **Commits.** The three main B runs record three commits (`ddd0abe…` Opus product, `dea2e12…` Qwen, `37655e1…` Opus plain). The prompt and playbook hashes match across product runs, but the validator, review and parsing code may differ. This could not be checked without git, which was out of scope. The frozen run must use one commit.
- **The playbook itself.** NDA-01's tiers overlap (written into the write-up's to-fix list, but not yet into the mapping). Several acceptable tiers ("silent") make most of B's rules unfalsifiable on recall. The playbook was written and calibrated with Claude (spec section 6 disclosure).
- **Gemini excluded quietly.** Its 21-NDA partial result appears in REPORT and not in the write-up. That is fine, but its high-effort setup through an agent harness should be described as a different kind of setup if it returns.
